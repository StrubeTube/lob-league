The problem

Selling out is free. That’s the bug.

Under the current rules, a team that falls out of the race is supposed to sell — it’s the rational play. The rulebook makes it free:

📉

Quitting costs nothing

A season goes sideways and the smart move is to sell. Future picks cost nothing this year — and giving up your stars costs nothing next year either, because the roster resets at the draft anyway. There is no downside to nuking your team.

🚀

One trade flips the race

A contender absorbs a playoff-caliber roster for futures. Months of drafting and managing get outweighed by a single transaction, and the standings warp for every team on the schedule.

🛡️

League integrity takes the hit

Moving the deadline up was our attempt to protect the race. The sell-off just happens earlier now — the standings still get warped by one-sided deals, and we gave up trading weeks to get there. The integrity of the season needs real protection, not a shorter calendar.

The fix

Three numbers. That’s the whole rulebook.

$30 → $2
Every player’s salary = his draft round. Round 1 costs $30, round 16 costs $2. No negotiating, no math — the board sets the price automatically.
The cap. No trade may leave your roster above it. Stack stars if you can fit them — the arms race finally has a ceiling.
The floor. No trade may leave your roster below it. Rebuilding and selling are still allowed — you just can’t nuke your roster to nothing. Every team stays a real team all season.
The price board

Keep a guy and he costs his draft round the first year, then climbs a round every year after — stars get expensive, bargains stay fun. Waivers cost $0, so nobody ever gets stuck unable to field a lineup.

Before you push back

What does NOT change

Fair warning: this reads like “more rules.” It’s actually three guardrails on the outside of the game you already play. Everything inside stays:

  1. Draft night is identical. Same board, same snake, same picks. Salaries are assigned by round automatically — you never set a price.
  2. Keeping is identical in spirit. You still keep your guys; the price just climbs a round a year like it always effectively did.
  3. Trading gets MORE room, not less. With the band doing the policing, we can push the trade deadline back instead of up — more weeks of live trading, because fire sales become structurally impossible instead of just early. In the replayed seasons most everyday trades pass untouched; the band only bites deals where one side stops being a football team.
  4. You do zero math. This site prices every roster live, and the Trade Tester gives any deal a verdict before you even propose it — and tells you exactly how to fix an illegal one.
